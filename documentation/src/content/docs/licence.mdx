## What it costs

Nothing.

And the reason is not a promise, it is the licence. LO-TEK is released under MIT, and **every version
that has been released stays MIT**. That permission is irrevocable. It cannot be withdrawn by anyone,
not even by me, and it also applies if the project is one day put on hold.

So you do not need to trust that I mean well. You can read the licence file.

## What you are allowed to do

All the usual things, without asking:

- use LO-TEK commercially, in as many projects as you like
- change it, and keep the changes to yourself
- ship it in a product you sell, including a closed one
- publish your own version of it

The only condition is that the copyright notice and the licence text are included in copies of
the software. They are in `node_modules`, so in practice this happens by itself.

You owe me no credit beyond that, no link back, and no notice that you
use it.

## What is not promised

MIT disclaims all warranty, and that is not a formality here.

In concrete terms:

- no guarantee that anything works
- no obligation for me to reply, fix bugs or release new versions
- no timeline, and no promise of backwards compatibility beyond what
  the [version contract](https://github.com/MarkusAugust/lo-tek/blob/master/interfaces/CHANGELOG.md)
  says

If you are considering LO-TEK for something that must work in five years, read that section once more. The licence makes
the code yours forever, but it does not make me your supplier.

## The name is not included

MIT gives away the code. It does not give away the name.

**LO-TEK** and the logo are not part of the permission. You can fork the code and do what you like with
it, but you cannot call the result LO-TEK, publish a package under that name, or give the
impression that it comes from here.

That is the usual arrangement in open source, and it exists as much for your sake as for mine:
the name meaning one specific thing is what makes it possible to say what you have actually adopted.

## Contributing

**Issues are always welcome.** A bug report, a question or an idea costs you nothing to
send and me nothing to read.

**Pull requests are accepted after a conversation first.** Say in an issue what you want to do, before you
write the code. That is not a barrier, it is a courtesy: LO-TEK has six checks that
enforce things that are not obvious from the outside, and I would rather say so before you have spent an evening
than after.

Contributors sign the
[contributor licence agreement](https://github.com/MarkusAugust/lo-tek/blob/master/CLA.md). You keep
the copyright to what you write. The agreement gives me the right to license the contribution on other terms
later, so that one contribution does not lock the project's future. Everything already published under
MIT stays MIT whatever happens.

## Forking

Feel free to fork.

If you need something LO-TEK does not do, disagree with a decision, or want to take it somewhere I am not
going to take it: take your copy and go. It needs no permission and no justification.

That you can carry on without me is not a weakness of the project. It is the whole point of giving it
away under MIT. The only thing I ask is that what you make gets its own name.
